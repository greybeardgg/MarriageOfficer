# Registration-side shot list

Agreed with Ryan on 11 September 2026. The front door now splits into two
counters of equal weight, and the wedding side has two hundred photographs
behind it while the registration side has one. This list closes that gap.

Every shot below is an **object or a document**, not a couple. That is
deliberate: it keeps the registration side honest without needing a client to
sign anything, and it means the whole list can be shot on a phone in an
afternoon, in daylight, on a table.

## How to shoot them

- **Daylight, indirect.** A table near a window, mid-morning. No flash, no lamp.
- **From directly above**, phone parallel to the table, for anything flat.
- **A plain surface.** Wood, or the grey-green of a desk mat. Not white.
- **Leave room around the object.** Do not fill the frame; the design crops.
- **Landscape and portrait of each.** The same object gets used at two shapes.
- **Nothing identifying.** Cover or blank out any real name, ID number,
  reference number or signature. POPIA applies to these images the moment a
  real person's details are legible.

## The shots

| # | Subject | Why the site needs it |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | The marriage register, open, on a table, a pen laid across the page | The single most distinctive object in the whole business. This is what "getting married" actually is. Wanted for the registration counter and the result screen's close. |
| 02 | The register closed, from above, showing the cover and its spine | A quieter alternative for the same slots. |
| 03 | The handwritten marriage certificate the couple leaves with, blanked | The thing people actually ask about. Pairs with the clause explaining what it is and what the unabridged one is for. |
| 04 | The unabridged certificate beside the handwritten one | The price clause for the unabridged certificate has nothing to show. This is that picture. |
| 05 | Your officer's stamp and its pad, from above, stamp resting on its side | The site's whole visual language is the stamp. One real one anchors it. |
| 06 | A fresh stamp impression on plain paper, from directly above | Wanted as a texture. Shoot three or four impressions at different pressures. |
| 07 | Two green barcoded ID books and one smart card, fanned | "What to bring on the day" is four clauses of text with no picture at all. |
| 08 | A passport open at a visa page beside an SA ID | For the 13% of enquirers with one non-South African partner, who face the hardest process and currently see nothing that looks like them. |
| 09 | A decree of divorce, folded, corner visible only | For the divorced clause. Fold it so nothing is readable. |
| 10 | Two pens and two witness chairs at an empty table | The room before anyone arrives. Useful as the quiet frame before the result screen's close. |
| 11 | Your office's signing table, wide, empty, daylight | Registration "at our offices" is a price line with nothing behind it. |
| 12 | A hand signing the register, from the signer's side, no face | The only shot with a person in it. A hand is not identifying, so no consent question arises. |

## Where each one lands

The build already has the slots. Nothing new needs designing when these
arrive: 01 or 02 replaces the registration counter's photograph, 03 and 04 go
beside the certificate clauses, 05 and 06 become the real texture behind the
stamps, 07 and 08 go into "What to bring on the day", and 11 goes
into the price section beside what registration at the offices includes.

## The alternative

If a shoot is not going to happen soon, these can all be generated to
production quality instead, on a machine with image generation available. That
takes one setting (`OPENAI_API_KEY`) and about twenty minutes. Real objects
photographed on a real table will still look better, because they will be your
register and your stamp.
